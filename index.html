PhD at a glance

Flexible Job Shop Scheduling with Human-Resource Side Constraints

Classical flexible job shop models treat machines as the only scarce resource and assume operators are always available. On real shop floors, setups, quality control, assembly and machine supervision need skilled people, and their skills, shifts and performance decide whether a schedule can actually run. My thesis closes this gap in three complementary settings, each with its own exact and heuristic solution methods.

Supervisors: Prof. dr. El-Houssaine Aghezzaf & Prof. dr. Pieter Leyman

Machines + operators Under review

Dual-resource constrained flexible job shop

Each operation needs an eligible machine and a qualified operator, and is sequenced on both.

Methods: Logic-Based Benders Decomposition with CP and tabu search; a joint neighbourhood in which one move changes both the machine and the operator of an operation.

Average optimality gap cut from 37% to 6% on 262 benchmark instances; the standalone tabu search gets within 0.2% of one-hour CP solutions.

Instances, outputs & results
Operator timetabling Published

Integrated scheduling and operator shifts

Operators are assigned to machines and shifts under working-time rules, and every active machine shift needs a qualified operator.

Methods: strengthened MIP, LBBD with strengthened cuts, a matheuristic (LNS, local branching, fix-and-optimize) and an extended tabu search.

A decision-support toolbox matched to instance size, from exact LBBD to fast tabu search, being tested in industrial environments.

Uncertainty In progress

Stochastic flexible job shop

Processing times vary with human-related factors, so a schedule has to perform well across many realisations, not just on average.

Methods: two-stage stochastic programming with SAA; expected makespan, makespan service level and CVaR; Benders decomposition with critical-path cuts; a measure-aware stochastic tabu search.

Risk-aware schedules that account for unfavourable processing-time outcomes, with bounds from exact decomposition.

What I work on

Research Interests

Production Scheduling

Flexible job shop, flow shop and dual-resource (machine/operator) scheduling problems from real manufacturing settings.

Combinatorial Optimization

Mixed-integer programming and constraint programming models for large-scale discrete decision problems.

Exact & Decomposition Methods

Benders and Logic-Based Benders Decomposition, and hybrid MIP/CP schemes that make hard problems tractable.

Metaheuristics

Local search, tabu search and variable neighborhood search with graph-based neighborhood structures for high-quality solutions within tight time limits.

Optimization under Uncertainty

Two-stage stochastic programming, sample average approximation, L-shaped and Benders decompositions, risk measures such as CVaR, and stochastic heuristics.

Human-Centric & Sustainable Manufacturing

Operator skills, shift regulations and human-related variability in scheduling (Industry 5.0), plus sustainability objectives such as carbon emissions.
